# E1?E40: source proof and guidance audit

Source: [Euclid, Elements, Book I, Clark University edition](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/bookI.html). ## Meaning of source fidelity

The app keeps its point names and formal statement grammar. Equilateral-property, triangle-congruence, angle-decomposition, and equality lines sometimes split one sentence of Euclid into several checkable statements. Those extra lines explain the same argument; they do not replace it with a different theorem. Radius equality uses the circle definition (D15); editorial source links sometimes attach the center definition to the same inference. Unnumbered elementary order/substitution arguments appear as Deduction.

Contradictions are closed arguments on one justified conclusion line. Their hypothetical assumptions, cut points, comparisons, contradiction, and discharge are retained in guidance and right-click proof inspection. They are not accepted as unconditional proof premises or added to the actual construction. This preserves the app?s existing rule that full compound arguments are revealed on right-click.

The displayed E26 exercise supplies the opposite-side case and E28 supplies the corresponding-angle case. Their companion cases are separately tested against their own source hypotheses and routes; this audit does not introduce a new case selector.

## Shared corrections

- A source route can nominate an automatic justification, but the independent formal checker must validate it against the real construction and preceding accepted lines. The route supplies no mathematical facts, and cannot make a missing hypothesis true. Other valid statements and references still use ordinary inference.
- Vertical angles and substituting an equal angle are separate operations. The vertical-angle rule no longer silently performs the later transitivity step.
- A triangle theorem can conclude the corresponding angle, side, or area directly from its hypotheses; an extra congruence sentence is optional. Ray aliases use recorded incidence, not screen alignment.
- Corresponding-part references retain their actual triangle-comparison source. An independent SAS comparison can still establish the corresponding part when its own premises have been proved.
- Strict addition/subtraction must preserve an existing inequality by adding/subtracting matching nonnegative quantities. General equality substitution and strict transitivity use explicit deductions from posted comparisons.
- Produced lines can explicitly mark a continuation endpoint for a subsequent cutoff. The extension incidence is recorded, saved, and replayed from an operation record, rather than recovered from moved display coordinates.
- A lesson waits for its conclusion to be posted. Merely knowing it as an unstated consequence of a congruence or equality chain no longer makes guidance skip the concluding source statement. This is a conclusion requirement, not an intermediate-proof checklist.
- Learning mode and the hint button share the same current-step routine. Specific explanations take precedence over generic rule descriptions. Numbered reference answers remain hidden in guidance.

## Guidance wording and hover feedback

Learning mode and the hint button now use short actions and a brief ?Why? explanation. Routine prompts avoid repeated instructions and unnecessary technical wording. Imagined points in contradiction arguments remain clearly separate from construction steps; full formal arguments remain available on right-click. The statement sequence and grading rules are unchanged.

Hovering a statement highlights all its referenced points and labels, whole figures and areas, lines, and angles at 110% size. Area figures get a light fill and grow about their centers. Squared-length expressions highlight matching squares only when those squares have construction records or proved square properties. All effects clear on exit and leave saved proof/construction data unchanged. The interaction suite checks every supplied proof statement across all 48 lessons.
